\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) lose their primary absolute position source
whenever GNSS is jammed, spoofed, or occluded, and inertial or visual odometry
then drifts without bound~\cite{couturier2021review}. Absolute visual
localization against a geo-referenced orthophoto removes that drift, and the
dominant approach today is \emph{appearance} matching: learned cross-view
retrieval~\cite{zhu2022transgeo,wu2024camp,wu2025uavgeoloc,ji2025game4loc}
or local/dense feature matching followed by a geometric
solver~\cite{lindenberger2023lightglue,edstedt2024roma,deng2026airlock,ye2026anyvisloc}.
These methods are strong, but their evidence is a texture statistic, and
texture is precisely what changes between a UAV frame and a map captured years
earlier, in another season, by another sensor.

Building footprints are an attractive alternative signal: they are
long-lived, largely independent of illumination and season, and visible from
both platforms. The conference version of this work (M0) encoded each
building's constrained Delaunay triangles with a 5-D descriptor of interior
and maximal free-cone (Ekeland) angles and retrieved them from a satellite
index by $k$-NN voting. Our own rigorous evaluation of that paradigm
(Sec.~\ref{sec:why_m0}) is, however, sobering: although the true location is
covered by mapped buildings in more than $90\%$ of frames, the geometrically
correct satellite element is essentially never among the top-ranked
candidates, and city-scale localization error stays at the kilometre level.
The reason is information-theoretic rather than an implementation detail: a
single triangle or building shape carries a few bits and recurs thousands of
times across a city, so \emph{per-element} retrieval cannot be unique, no
matter how the descriptor is refined. The discriminative information lives in
the \emph{joint configuration} of many buildings---exactly what per-element
retrieval discards.

This paper therefore changes the unit of inference. Instead of retrieving
structural elements, we \textbf{register the entire observed building
structure to the map by optimizing a single geometric objective over the
similarity group} $\mathrm{Sim}(2)$. The objective combines a robust
smoothed-chamfer term on building boundaries and a signed-mask overlap term,
both normalized over the camera footprint, with heading and altitude priors.
Because both data terms are cross-correlations for a fixed rotation and scale,
the optimum over \emph{all} translations of a search window is found exactly
by FFT; a continuous refinement removes grid quantization; and the curvature of
the objective at the optimum yields a Laplace covariance. Upstream
segmentation uncertainty is propagated into the objective through a
\emph{structural ensemble} whose agreement defines per-point persistence
weights, and the MFCA/Ekeland descriptor of the conference paper survives in
a new role---as a building-shape verifier that disambiguates correlation peaks
on regular layouts. Finally, the structural evidence (peak distinctiveness,
Laplace uncertainty, number and spread of buildings, persistence, shape
agreement) is turned into a calibrated \emph{integrity} score that decides
when to abstain.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A formulation of raster-derived building-structure localization as
  exact global optimization over $\mathrm{Sim}(2)$ with footprint-normalized
  correlation (Proposition~\ref{prop:fft}), requiring no external vector map
  and no scene-specific training in the geometric stage.
\item Stage-wise propagation of segmentation uncertainty through a structural
  ensemble into persistence-weighted evidence, and a Laplace covariance of
  the resulting pose.
\item A vertex-coupled MFCA building signature used for shape verification of
  pose hypotheses.
\item A structural integrity model built only from observable geometric
  evidence, enabling calibrated abstention.
\item An evaluation on three real benchmarks with complementary roles---
  UAV-VisLoc~\cite{xu2024uavvisloc} (continuous-map localization),
  World-UAV~\cite{wu2025uavgeoloc} (rotation/scale stress) and
  AnyVisLoc~\cite{ye2026anyvisloc} (low-altitude multi-view)---with
  parameters frozen from UAV-VisLoc, an oracle ladder that separates
  image$\rightarrow$structure errors from structure$\rightarrow$location
  errors, and an explicit validity envelope.
\end{enumerate}

We deliberately avoid claims of universal appearance or viewpoint invariance:
the matcher is appearance-independent \emph{conditional on} structure recovery,
and we measure where that condition holds.
