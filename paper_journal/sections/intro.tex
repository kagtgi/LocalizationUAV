\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) lose their primary absolute position source
whenever GNSS is jammed, spoofed or occluded, and inertial or visual odometry
then drifts without bound~\cite{couturier2021review}. Absolute visual
localization against a geo-referenced orthophoto removes that drift. The
dominant approaches are learned: cross-view retrieval
networks~\cite{zhu2022transgeo,wu2024camp,wu2025uavgeoloc,ji2025game4loc} and
learned local or dense matchers followed by a geometric
solver~\cite{lindenberger2023lightglue,edstedt2024roma,deng2026airlock,ye2026anyvisloc}.
They are strong, but they require training data that match the deployment
domain, and their evidence is appearance, which changes between a UAV frame and
a map captured years earlier, in another season, by another sensor.

Man-made structure is an attractive alternative signal: building outlines,
road edges and walls are long-lived and are visible to both platforms as
straight edges. Edge representations are already known to reduce appearance
dependence in UAV--satellite matching---CAEVL~\cite{amadei2026caevl} feeds
Canny edge maps to a network trained on satellite imagery and retrieves the
best reference tile. Our conference work (M0) went further towards explicit
geometry: it segmented building footprints, triangulated them (CDT) and
retrieved triangles by a 5-D descriptor of interior and Ekeland free-cone
angles. A rigorous evaluation of that paradigm (Sec.~\ref{sec:why_m0}) shows
why it fails at city scale: the correct element is essentially never retrieved,
because a single triangle or footprint carries a few bits and recurs thousands
of times. Two lessons follow. First, the \emph{unit of inference} must be the
joint configuration of the observed structure, not the element. Second, the
learned building segmenter was the weakest link: it misses roofs and invents
buildings on roads, and is a domain-dependent learned component.

This paper therefore proposes a \textbf{training-free, edge-constrained
structural registration}. Both images are reduced to the same explicit
primitive---oriented straight line structure extracted with an a-contrario line
detector---and the whole query structure is registered to the map by optimizing
a single objective over the similarity group $\mathrm{Sim}(2)$: a masked,
orientation-stacked normalized cross-correlation between the query lines and
oriented smoothed-chamfer kernels of the map. Every term of this objective is a
cross-correlation, so for each rotation and scale the optimum over \emph{all}
translations of the search window is found exactly by FFT, and a rotation acts
on the query only by moving samples and relabelling orientation channels.
Extraction uncertainty is propagated by a structural ensemble whose agreement
weights each line sample. The top hypotheses are then verified on a sparse line
graph---junctions of the line structure, its constrained Delaunay
triangulation (the natural input of CDT is exactly points plus prescribed
segments), and the Ekeland free-cone angle at each junction---which carries the
conference descriptor forward in the role where it is informative:
disambiguating a small set of pose hypotheses rather than retrieving from
$10^5$--$10^6$ candidates. A continuous refinement, a Laplace covariance and an
integrity score built only from observable geometric evidence complete the
estimate.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A training-free formulation of UAV localization as exact global
  optimization over $\mathrm{Sim}(2)$ of a masked, orientation-stacked NCC
  between oriented line structure and oriented chamfer kernels
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:fft}); no learned component, no external vector map.
\item Propagation of line-extraction uncertainty through a structural ensemble
  into persistence-weighted evidence, and a Laplace covariance of the pose.
\item Line-graph verification of pose hypotheses with junction
  correspondence, topological consistency on the constrained Delaunay
  triangulation of the line graph, and the Ekeland free-cone angle.
\item A structural integrity model from observable geometric evidence for
  calibrated abstention.
\item An evaluation on UAV-VisLoc~\cite{xu2024uavvisloc},
  World-UAV~\cite{wu2025uavgeoloc} and AnyVisLoc~\cite{ye2026anyvisloc} with
  parameters frozen from UAV-VisLoc, an oracle-structure ladder separating
  extraction errors from registration errors, and the validity envelope of
  planar edge structure.
\end{enumerate}

We avoid claims of universal appearance or viewpoint invariance: registration
is appearance-independent \emph{conditional on} the extraction of line
structure, and we measure where that condition holds.
