\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a geo-referenced, north-up orthophoto with ground sampling
distance $g_S$, and $I$ a single UAV image with an optional pose prior:
altitude $h$, heading $\psi$ and a coarse position $\bar{\mathbf{p}}$ from
inertial or visual odometry. We seek the UAV's map position
$\hat{\mathbf{p}}\in\mathbb{R}^2$, its covariance $\hat\Sigma$ and an integrity
score $c\in[0,1]$, or an abstention. On the ground plane the image--map relation
is a similarity $T=(s,\theta,\mathbf t)\in\mathrm{Sim}(2)$,
\begin{equation}
  T(\mathbf x)=\tfrac{s}{g}R_\theta\mathbf x+\mathbf t,
  \label{eq:sim2}
\end{equation}
mapping a metric point $\mathbf x$ of the north-up, metric-canonicalized query
(origin at its centre) to a map pixel at working resolution $g$; the position
is $\hat{\mathbf p}=T(\mathbf 0)$. The residual scale $s$ and rotation $\theta$
absorb altitude and heading errors. The planar model is exact for nadir views
of flat terrain; its validity envelope under tilt and relief is measured, not
assumed (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_anyvisloc}). Table~\ref{tab:notation} lists the notation.

\section{Method: Training-free Edge-constrained Structural Registration}
\label{sec:method}

The method uses no learned component (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Both images are
reduced to the same explicit geometric primitive---\emph{oriented straight line
structure} (building outlines, road and parcel edges, walls)---and the query
structure is registered to the map by exact global optimization over
$\mathrm{Sim}(2)$. A line graph built on the same primitives (junctions and a
constrained Delaunay triangulation) then verifies the best hypotheses
topologically, using the Ekeland free-cone angle of the conference descriptor,
and yields a robust continuous refinement. Crucially, \emph{no structural
element is ever retrieved}: per-element retrieval is the failure mode of M0
(Sec.~\ref{sec:why_m0}); elements are only compared \emph{after} a global pose
has been hypothesized.

\subsection{Metric canonicalization}
\label{sec:canon}
The query is rotated north-up by the IMU heading $\psi$ and resampled to a
common metric resolution $g_0=0.3$\,m/px using $g_I=k\,h$. When intrinsics are
known, $k=2\tan(\mathrm{FOV}_v/2)/H_{\mathrm{px}}$; for UAV-VisLoc, whose metadata
lack intrinsics, $k$ is estimated once on a calibration site excluded from the
evaluation and frozen. A footprint mask $V$ records the observed ground.
Residual heading and scale errors are left to the optimizer.

\subsection{Oriented line structure and persistence}
\label{sec:lines}
Straight segments are detected with LSD~\cite{vongioi2010lsd}, which controls
false detections a~contrario and has no trained parameters. Only segments at
least $\ell_{\min}$ long are kept, which removes most texture edges; what
remains is dominated by man-made structure. Each segment is rasterized at the
working resolution $g$ with a label $b\in\{1,\dots,K\}$, the bin of its
orientation in $[0,\pi)$ (we use $K=8$, i.e.\ $22.5^\circ$ bins).

\paragraph{Uncertainty propagation.} Image-derived structure is uncertain: a
small change of contrast or resolution can split, merge or remove a segment.
We therefore run the detector on a \emph{structural ensemble}
$\mathcal E$ of the query (image scale $\in\{0.75,1,1.33\}$ $\times$
pre-smoothing $\in\{0,1\}$\,px) and define for every nominal boundary sample
$\mathbf x_b$ its persistence
\begin{equation}
 p_b=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal E|}\textstyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal E}
 \mathbb 1\bigl[d(\mathbf x_b,\partial E)\le r\bigr],\qquad r=1.5\,\mathrm m,
 \label{eq:persistence}
\end{equation}
used as its weight $w_b=p_b$. Structure that does not survive plausible
extraction changes is down-weighted before it can influence the pose.

\subsection{Oriented kernel maps}
\label{sec:refmaps}
For each orientation channel $k$ the map is summarized by a smoothed-chamfer
kernel
\begin{equation}
 G_k(\mathbf u)=\max\!\bigl(\kappa_k(\mathbf u),\tfrac12\kappa_{k\pm1}(\mathbf u)\bigr),
 \qquad \kappa_k(\mathbf u)=\exp\!\bigl(-D_k(\mathbf u)^2/2\sigma^2\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $D_k$ is the distance transform of the map lines in bin $k$ and the
neighbouring bins give a soft $\pm22.5^\circ$ orientation tolerance
($\sigma=2$\,m). This is the directional-chamfer
idea~\cite{shotton2008contour,liu2010dcm}: a query line is rewarded only for
landing near a map line \emph{of the same direction}, which removes the dominant
source of false alignments in line-rich scenes (a road edge aligning with a
perpendicular building wall).

\subsection{Objective: masked, orientation-stacked NCC}
\label{sec:objective}
A query sample $\mathbf x_b$ with orientation $\phi_b$ votes, under pose $T$, in
channel $k_b(\theta)=\mathrm{bin}(\phi_b+\theta)$. The data term is the
normalized cross-correlation between the weighted query samples and the kernel
maps, computed over the \emph{stacked} domain of all channels and restricted to
the overlap $\Omega_T$ between the camera footprint and the valid map:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
 J(T)=\frac{\sum_b w_b\,G_{k_b(\theta)}(T\mathbf x_b)-\bar w\,\bar G_{\Omega_T}\,|\Omega_T|K}
 {\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega_T}(w)\,\bigl(\mathrm{Var}_{\Omega_T}(G)+\epsilon^2\bigr)}\;|\Omega_T|K}
 -\lambda\Bigl[\tfrac{(\theta-\theta_0)^2}{2\sigma_\theta^2}+\tfrac{(\log s-\log s_0)^2}{2\sigma_s^2}\Bigr]\;}
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where the means and variances are taken over $\Omega_T$ and all $K$ channels.
Normalization makes the score invariant to the local density of structure, so
that busy districts are not preferred over sparse ones merely for containing
more lines (the ``impostor density'' effect of per-element voting), and
restricting to $\Omega_T$ makes footprint parts outside a small map neither
help nor hurt.

\subsection{Exact global search}
\label{sec:fft}
\begin{proposition}[Exact translation search]
\label{prop:fft}
For fixed $(\theta,s)$ every sum in \eqref{eq:objective}---the correlation
term $\sum_k(\mathcal T_k\star G_k)$, the overlap size, and the first and second
moments of the query weights and of the map over the overlap---is a
cross-correlation of a template (query weights per channel, their square, the
footprint) with a map raster (the $G_k$, $\sum_kG_k$, $\sum_kG_k^2$, the valid
mask). Hence $J$ is evaluated at every integer translation of the search window
with $O(K N\log N)$ operations, and summing the $K$ channel spectra before one
inverse transform keeps the cost of the correlation term independent of $K$ in
the inverse step.
\end{proposition}
A rotation acts on the query only by rotating positions and relabelling
channels ($k_b\mapsto\mathrm{bin}(\phi_b+\theta)$), so the whole $\mathrm{Sim}(2)$
grid is enumerated exactly: $\theta\in[-10^\circ,10^\circ]$ step $2.5^\circ$
with an IMU heading, $[0,360^\circ)$ without, and five scales. Templates are
generated per GPU batch, the running maximum over $(\theta,s)$ is kept per
translation, the top-$k$ peaks are extracted (non-maximum suppression 30\,m),
and the best score more than 50\,m away defines the runner-up $J_2$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:grid} bounds the discretization error in $(\theta,s)$.
Search windows are a disk of radius $R$ around $\bar{\mathbf p}$ (prior mode) or
the whole map (global mode, tiled).

\subsection{Line-graph verification with the Ekeland free-cone angle}
\label{sec:verify}
Correlation can alias on regular layouts. We therefore verify the top
hypotheses on a sparse graph built from the same primitives. Segments are
filtered structurally (collinear fragments merged; dense parallel hatching such
as roof ribs and crop rows suppressed), and \emph{junctions} are formed where
two non-parallel segments meet (L, T, X types; angle $>30^\circ$). The junctions
and the segments between them form a planar straight-line graph, which is the
native input of a constrained Delaunay triangulation~\cite{shewchuk1996triangle}.
At each junction the \emph{Ekeland free-cone angle} $e_v$---the largest
obstacle-free angular sector between its incident constrained edges, the
quantity of the conference descriptor---is rotation- and scale-invariant.

Given a hypothesis $T$, a query junction $i$ is paired with the nearest map
junction $j$ if (i) $\|T\mathbf J_i-\mathbf J_j\|\le3$\,m, (ii) all its incident
directions, rotated by $\theta$, have a map counterpart within $12^\circ$, and
(iii) $|e_i-e_j|\le30^\circ$. The verification score
\begin{equation}
 A(T)=\rho\,\Bigl(\tfrac12+\tfrac12\tau\Bigr)\Bigl(\tfrac12+\tfrac12\,\overline{\exp(-|e_i-e_j|/\sigma_e)}\Bigr)
\end{equation}
combines the matched fraction $\rho$, the topological consistency $\tau$ (the
fraction of structural edges between matched query junctions that are also
edges of the map graph), and Ekeland agreement ($\sigma_e=20^\circ$). Peaks
are re-ranked by $J+\gamma A$. Because the comparison happens only between
elements that the global pose already brings within metres of each other, the
discriminability ceiling of per-element retrieval does not apply.

\subsection{Continuous refinement and uncertainty}
\label{sec:refine}
From the selected hypothesis, (a) $J$ is maximized over continuous
$\mathbf q=(t_u,t_v,\theta,\log s)$ with bilinear sampling in space and linear,
cyclic interpolation across orientation channels, and (b) a Sim(2) is fitted
by RANSAC~\cite{fischler1981ransac} to the verified junction correspondences
(Umeyama on the inliers). The Laplace approximation
\begin{equation}
 \hat\Sigma_{\mathbf q}=\bigl(-\tfrac{N}{\tau}\nabla^2_{\mathbf q}J(\hat{\mathbf q})\bigr)^{-1},\qquad
 \hat\Sigma_{\mathbf p}=g^2[\hat\Sigma_{\mathbf q}]_{1:2,1:2}
 \label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
is computed with central finite differences (bilinear interpolation has
vanishing second derivatives).

\subsection{Structural integrity and abstention}
\label{sec:integrity}
The evidence vector
$\mathbf z=(J_1-J_2,\,J_1,\,\log\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}\hat\Sigma_{\mathbf p}},\,
\log(1+n_{\mathrm{struct}}),\,\bar p,\,A,\,n_{\mathrm{inl}})$ contains only
observable quantities: peak distinctiveness, local precision, the amount and
persistence of observed structure, graph agreement and RANSAC support. A
logistic model $c=\sigma(\boldsymbol\beta^\top\mathbf z)$, fitted
leave-one-site-out, predicts success at threshold $T$; the system abstains
when $c<\tau_c$. Confidence is thus derived from the observability and
uniqueness of the geometric evidence rather than from a learned score.

\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}
Offline: one line detection and $K$ distance transforms per map (linear in map
area) and one graph construction. Online: line detection on the query
ensemble, $|\Theta||\mathcal S|$ batched FFT correlations of the window, a
graph verification that is linear in the number of query junctions, and a
constant-size refinement. Runtime does not grow with the number of similar
structures in the map, unlike descriptor retrieval.
